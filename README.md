# Cycling Network Connectivity and Accessibility in Treviso
### A GIS-Based Assessment and Prioritisation of Missing Links

## Project Overview

This independent study investigates the connectivity, continuity and accessibility of cycling infrastructure in Treviso and its surrounding municipalities. Using Geographic Information Systems (GIS), the project identifies discontinuities in the existing cycling network, evaluates their potential impact on urban and intermunicipal connectivity, and establishes a prioritisation framework for future infrastructure improvements.

The analysis focuses on identifying missing links, assessing their strategic relevance and highlighting opportunities to improve the continuity of cycling routes between residential areas, urban centres and key destinations.

## Methodology

The study was conducted using **QGIS**, combining spatial data processing, cycling network mapping and a structured assessment of identified infrastructure gaps.

The methodology includes:
- Mapping existing cycling infrastructure and identifying network discontinuities.
- Identifying and classifying 65 infrastructure gaps across the study area.
- Assessing discontinuities according to their severity and their relevance to local and intermunicipal connectivity.
- Developing a prioritisation framework to identify the most strategically relevant interventions.
- Producing thematic maps to communicate spatial patterns and infrastructure deficiencies.

## Key Results

The assessment identified 65 cycling network discontinuities, of which 48 were classified as high-priority interventions.

The findings highlight recurring infrastructure issues, including cycling paths that terminate abruptly, incomplete connections to railway stations and discontinuities affecting access to important destinations.

The analysis provides a spatially structured basis for prioritising future investments in cycling infrastructure and improving network connectivity across municipal boundaries.

## Tools and Technologies

- **QGIS:** Spatial analysis, network mapping and cartographic visualisation.
- **OpenStreetMap:** Geospatial infrastructure data.

ISTAT: Administrative and territorial reference data.

Project Scope and Limitations

This is an independent, planning-oriented GIS assessment. The prioritisation framework represents an analytical evaluation of infrastructure gaps rather than a detailed engineering design or a formal cost-benefit analysis.

The results are intended to support preliminary infrastructure planning and identify areas where further technical investigation may be warranted.
